# Acute chest pain — ACS versus inflammatory myopericardial syndrome

Status: DRAFT — unreviewed teaching pilot, not clinical guidance.

## Q0 — Clinical problem
A patient presents with acute chest pain. The first task is to assess instability and time-sensitive life-threatening diagnoses, rather than prematurely label a characteristic symptom as pericarditis. This module tests how the editorial system distinguishes observation, mechanism, and decision.

## Q1 — Mechanism
ACS concerns myocardial ischemia and possible injury from a coronary supply problem. Pericarditis concerns inflammation of the pericardium and adjacent epicardial involvement; myocarditis concerns inflammatory myocardial involvement. Similar pain, ECG changes and troponin abnormalities may arise from different causal pathways. Troponin signifies myocardial injury but does not by itself establish its cause.

## Q2 — Evidence and decisions
The 2025 ESC myocarditis/pericarditis guideline frames overlapping presentations as inflammatory myopericardial syndrome (IMPS). Source: [[source:ESC2025-IMPS]]. The 2015 ESC pericarditis guideline is historical and superseded: [[source:ESC2015-PERICARDIAL]]. Before publishing a diagnostic decision algorithm, verify the complete 2025 diagnostic criteria, risk stratification tables, clinical evaluation recommendations and original figures. The registered 2025 official slide set is [[source:ESC2025-SLIDES]].

No particular ECG morphology, symptom description, or troponin value alone can substitute for an integrated assessment. This draft does not provide treatment orders.

## Q3 — Falsification and reassessment
A change in clinical stability, evolving ECG findings, new myocardial dysfunction, or new evidence about the underlying mechanism requires revisiting the initial hypothesis and checking downstream conclusions. The same diagnostic label cannot be assumed to represent the same physiology in all patients.
