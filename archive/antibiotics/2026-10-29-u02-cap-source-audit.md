## Primary original case verified

Spretz MW, Orrukem MM, Khwaja B, Karnath B. *Legionella pneumonia: A Case of Fever Prolonged for 10 Days*. Cureus. 2024 May 9;16(5):e59979. DOI 10.7759/cureus.59979; full publisher HTML https://www.cureus.com/articles/251787-legionella-pneumonia-a-case-of-fever-prolonged-for-10-days ; backup PMC https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11162253/ .

Original case presentation/Table 1/Figures checked as publisher article text (not screenshot of images; figures not reproduced). Patient: **69M**, 10 days fever and dyspnea, later diarrhea/nausea; HTN, treated HCV, past oral SCC resection. At arrival T101.8F, HR79, RR18, BP126/71, SpO2 91%, bilateral crackles, CXR multifocal R>L; Cr3.3, Na134, AST141, ALT117. Authors state CURB-65 score 2. Viruses COVID/flu/RSV negative. Early therapy IV ceftriaxone (Rocephin)+PO azithromycin; Legionella/pneumococcal urine antigens sent. Persistently febrile and dependent on 3L/min O2, SpO2 89% when attempting wean. Blood cultures no growth hospital day 3. Cr1.94 at time of contrast chest CT; CT multifocal consolidative ground glass and increased small parapneumonic effusion. Cefepime begun after CT. Hospital day 4 family reported recent stagnant-water attic cleaning; outside-lab Legionella UAT positive. Antibiotics tailored to levofloxacin; improvement over subsequent course, >48h afebrile before discharge; authors report total ~10-day antibiotic course and outpatient Cr1.20 at 1 month.

**Not reported / cannot infer:** details of oxygen during initial SpO2, causative serogroup/genotype, Legionella isolate AST/MIC, the counterfactual response if azithromycin continued, actual Legionella macrolide resistance, baseline Cr, whether effusion was infected or needed drainage, exact original prescribing rationale beyond what authors stated, total time from empiric initiation to stable condition. Do not convert an observed temporal association into fluoroquinolone superiority, and do not mistake higher-spectrum cefepime for proven anti-Legionella action.

## Guideline and evidence roles

1. Metlay JP et al. 2019 ATS/IDSA CAP https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6812437/ (official landing https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/community-acquired-pneumonia-cap-in-adults ). Roles: nonsevere inpatient empiric beta-lactam + macrolide vs respiratory FQ; risk-gated MRSA/Pseudomonas empiric add-on; stop routine HCAP category; prior respiratory MRSA/Pseudomonas isolate and recent hospitalization + parenteral antibiotic exposure; 48h culture-guided de-escalation with improvement; severe-CAP/travel/outbreak Legionella testing. **Guideline cohort ≠ index patient's proven clinical reasoning.**
2. Jones BE et al. ATS CAP update published online Jul 18 2025, printed AJRCCM 2026;212:24-44 DOI 10.1164/rccm.202507-1692ST https://academic.oup.com/ajrccm/article/212/1/24/8435770 . Roles: virus-positive empirical antibiotics split by patient setting and severity (conditional, VERY LOW evidence); short course after clinical stability nonsevere (minimum 3 days, conditional/LOW evidence) vs severe (at least 5 days, strong/LOW evidence), exceptions in Table 1. **This is ATS 2025, not a joint endorsed 2025 ATS/IDSA update.**
3. IDSA statement by Klompas et al., 2025, https://academic.oup.com/cid/article/82/4/622/8364653 . Agreed with 8/10 recommendations but withheld endorsement due to two antibiotic recommendations in virus-positive adults with comorbidities/nonsevere admitted patients. Statement is position/reasoning, not new RCT proving withholding safe in every patient.
4. 2026 meta-analysis MRSA PCR https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41969753/ : supporting context for reassessment; high NPV depends on prevalence, patient population, pre-test risk. The manuscript avoids quoting a fixed sensitivity or applying nasal PCR to index patient.

## Deliberate article-level boundaries

- No synthetic labs, fictional order sheet, MICs, special antimicrobial doses, or undocumented patient actions.
- Infection source vs organism vs antibiotic activity vs patient clinical trajectory are **different questions**. Cultures/antigens answer only their targets.
- Symptoms at day 3 cannot diagnose antibacterial failure or resistance; more detailed differential must include active drug with inflammatory lag, inactive/inadequate treatment, local complications, or noninfectious co-pathology.
- A case report provides temporal observation; guideline provides conditional population recommendations; dissent is explicitly attributed to IDSA; neither identifies the isolated organism in any other future patient.
- Original figure content/captions visually **not independently screenshot-verified**. No figure or table reproductions were copied to the site.
